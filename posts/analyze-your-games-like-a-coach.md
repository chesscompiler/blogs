The difference between a club player and a master isn't just how they play, but how they **analyze**. Most players finish a game, check the engine's "eval" bar, and move on. To truly improve, you must dig deeper.

## 🚫 The "Engine Trap"

Using Stockfish immediately after a game is the fastest way to *not* learn. It tells you the "what" (this move is +1.5) but rarely the "why."

### The Coach's Method:
1. **Analyze without the engine first.** Write down what you were thinking during the game.
2. **Identify Critical Moments.** Where did the "tension" change? Where did you feel uncomfortable?
3. **Check your calculation.** Did you see the tactical line the engine eventually pointed out?

---

## 🛠️ Identifying Tactical vs. Strategic Errors

When you look back at a loss, categorize your mistakes:

### 1. The Tactical Oversight
Did you miss a simple fork or a discovered attack? This is a "vision" error.

*A common opening mistake: Forgetting about the pressure on c3.*

### 2. The Strategic Misconception
Did you trade your "good" bishop for their "bad" knight? This is a "knowledge" error.

---

## 📝 The Analysis Checklist

Every time you review a game, answer these three questions:
1. **At what point did I stop following my opening prep?**
2. **What was my opponent's main threat that I ignored?**
3. **If I could replay one move, which would it be and why?**

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** If you lost because of a specific tactical pattern, find 10 puzzles on that pattern and solve them immediately. This "Corrective Training" is what coaches use to fix weaknesses.

---

## 🎓 Learning from the Engine (The Right Way)

Once you've done your own work, turn on the engine.
- Look for **"Silent Moves"**—non-forcing moves that the engine loves but you ignored.
- Use the engine to verify if your "sacrifices" were sound or just "hope chess."
