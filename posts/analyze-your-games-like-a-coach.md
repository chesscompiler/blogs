Post-game analysis is where improvement becomes measurable. Players waste this step by opening an engine too early and collecting moves without lessons. Coaching-style analysis starts with your thinking, your evaluations, and the moments where your process broke down.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Separate human analysis from engine verification.
- Identify turning points, not just blunders.
- Track repeated decision-making problems such as time trouble, opening confusion, or poor threat awareness.
- Turn one analyzed game into a concrete study task for the next week.

## Practical Framework
- First replay the game without an engine and mark the moments where you were unsure.
- Write what you saw, what you feared, and what you chose.
- Only after that compare your notes to engine suggestions and stronger plans.
- Finish by extracting one opening note, one middlegame lesson, and one training drill.

## Model Position 1: Mark the critical moment, not every move

*A game is usually decided by a few key choices, not by every normal move.*

When you analyze, pause at the moments where the evaluation or the plan changed. That is where the real lesson is. Treating every move equally hides the important decision.

## Model Position 2: Compare your evaluation to reality

<chess-board fen="r2q1rk1/pp2bppp/2n1pn2/2bp4/3P4/2NBPN2/PPQ2PPP/R1B2RK1 w - - 0 10" highlight="c5,d4,e3" arrows="d4-c5,c6-b4"></chess-board>
*The largest training gains often come from evaluation errors, not tactical errors.*

Maybe you thought the position was equal while the structure already favored one side. When your evaluation is wrong, your move choices will often be wrong even if the tactics are fine.

## Common Mistakes
- Checking the engine immediately and never recording your own thoughts.
- Annotating only blunders and missing the strategic turning points.
- Keeping no record of repeated mistakes across many games.
- Finishing analysis without a specific training action.

## Training Work
- Analyze every serious game in three passes: memory, board analysis, then engine verification.
- Maintain a mistake log with categories such as tactics, planning, clock use, and psychology.
- At the end of each week, choose the most common mistake type and train that directly.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Good analysis changes future behavior. If a game review does not produce a specific training task, it is not finished.
