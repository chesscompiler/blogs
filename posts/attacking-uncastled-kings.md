An uncastled king is vulnerable because the center is still where the pieces and pawns collide. The right punishment is usually direct and central: open files, gain time by development, and force the king to lose coordination before it finds shelter.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Recognize when a king in the center is temporarily awkward and when it is genuinely in danger.
- Use central breaks, pins, and development tempos to keep the king exposed.
- Understand why open files matter more than flank gestures in these positions.
- Know when to sacrifice material to tear open the central files.

## Practical Framework
- If the enemy king is uncastled, search for forcing operations in the center first.
- Use piece development with tempo so the attack grows while your position improves.
- Open one file completely rather than half-opening three different ones with no follow-up.
- Keep queens and major pieces on the board unless a tactical reason favors simplification.

## Model Position 1: Strike before the king escapes

*Opening the center is often the cleanest punishment for delayed castling.*

The central break matters because it attacks the king and opens lines for the rest of the army at the same time. Every tempo counts before the defender can castle or force exchanges.

## Model Position 2: Pins make central kings feel even more cramped

<chess-board fen="r1bqk2r/pppp1ppp/2n2n2/2b1p3/2B1P3/2N2N2/PPPP1PPP/R1BQ1RK1 b kq - 6 5" highlight="f7,e8,f6" arrows="c4-f7"></chess-board>
*Targets near the king are stronger when development already points at them.*

Uncastled kings hate pins because every pinned defender doubles as a tactical and strategic problem. It becomes harder to complete development and harder to untangle under pressure.

## Common Mistakes
- Attacking the wrong wing when the king is still in the center.
- Rushing into sacrifices without enough pieces developed.
- Trading queens reflexively and letting the king off the hook.
- Forgetting that the opponent may solve all problems with one accurate defensive move.

## Training Work
- Review opening games where one side delayed castling and identify the exact move that opened the center.
- Play training positions where your only task is to punish the uncastled king quickly and cleanly.
- In analysis, compare your attacking move to the strongest central break available.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Against an uncastled king, speed and central force are more important than decorative attacking ideas. Open the center before the chance disappears.
