Winning a position and winning the game are not the same task. Conversion demands discipline: reduce counterplay, choose the right exchanges, improve the king, and keep enough accuracy that the opponent never gets practical chances back.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Recognize when simplification helps and when it kills your winning chances.
- Reduce the opponent's active resources before grabbing more material.
- Use king activity and piece coordination to make the advantage durable.
- Convert by method instead of trying to finish with one final tactic.

## Practical Framework
- Ask what counterplay the opponent still has and remove that first.
- Trade into a winning endgame, not just into fewer pieces.
- Improve your worst piece and activate your king before starting unnecessary adventures.
- When ahead materially, avoid moves that create new weaknesses unless they win by force.

## Model Position 1: Centralize the king and cut counterplay

*Many winning positions are converted by improving the king one step at a time.*

Players throw away wins because they rush. The king often does the quiet technical work that makes the rest of the win easy and safe.

## Model Position 2: Trade active pieces before chasing pawns

<chess-board fen="6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/5PK1/5P1P/6R1 w - - 0 1" highlight="g7,h7,f7,g1" arrows="g1-c1"></chess-board>
*The best winning move may be a restriction move, not a capture.*

When you are better, every trade should be judged by what remains. If the opponent's only hope is an active rook or a passed pawn, remove that hope before collecting more material.

## Common Mistakes
- Forcing tactics when a simple technical line wins safely.
- Trading into an ending you have not actually evaluated.
- Ignoring the opponent's active piece because you are focused on extra pawns.
- Rushing pawns without king support.

## Training Work
- Replay games you won and games you failed to convert, then compare the quality of your simplification decisions.
- Practice basic winning endgames from both sides so you understand what the defender is trying to do.
- In a winning position, force yourself to name the opponent's last active resource before moving.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Clean conversion is an act of respect. You respect the opponent's counterplay enough to remove it before you celebrate the win.
