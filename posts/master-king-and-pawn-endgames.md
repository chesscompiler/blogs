King and pawn endings look simple because there are few pieces on the board. In reality they punish inaccurate thinking more than almost any other phase. One tempo, one opposition mistake, or one wrong king route can flip the result completely.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Use opposition, triangulation, and key squares as practical tools rather than textbook buzzwords.
- Know when an outside passed pawn wins because it distracts the king.
- Count races accurately and understand the square of the pawn.
- Recognize when a king-and-pawn ending should be entered from a heavier ending.

## Practical Framework
- In pure pawn endings, king activity is the first priority and pawn moves are precious.
- Count key squares before pushing passed pawns.
- Use opposition to force the enemy king back or to seize the critical route.
- If the position is a race, calculate all the way to promotion, not halfway.

## Model Position 1: Opposition decides who enters first

*The side that gains the opposition often gains the route to the key squares.*

Opposition is not a ritual. It is a way of forcing the enemy king to yield ground. Once you see that purpose, the right move becomes much easier to find.

## Model Position 2: The outside passed pawn distracts the king

<chess-board fen="8/8/8/2k5/8/2K5/P7/7P w - - 0 1" highlight="a2,h2,a8,h8" arrows="a2-a4,h2-h4"></chess-board>
*Passed pawns on the edge matter because kings cannot be in two places at once.*

The outside passer often wins not by promoting immediately, but by pulling the enemy king far enough away for your own king to invade on the other side.

## Common Mistakes
- Pushing pawns automatically instead of using the king first.
- Entering a king-and-pawn ending without counting the race to the end.
- Misusing opposition by memorizing the term rather than the purpose.
- Ignoring reserve tempi in pawn endings.

## Training Work
- Practice basic opposition positions until you can explain them without looking at notes.
- Set up pawn races and calculate them before moving any piece.
- From your own games, mark every heavy-piece ending that could have been simplified into a winning pawn ending.

## Why This Chapter Matters
King and pawn endings teach pure logic. If you understand them, your judgment in more complex endings improves immediately.
