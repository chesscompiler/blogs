Minor-piece endgames are often decided long before the final tactic. A knight that owns outposts or a bishop that dominates both wings changes what pawns can safely do. Understanding piece quality is the center of these endings.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Judge bishop versus knight endgames by pawn structure and available squares.
- Recognize when a knight outpost is worth more than a healthy pawn majority.
- Use king activity to support the stronger minor piece.
- Know when opposite-colored bishops are drawish and when they are dangerous.

## Practical Framework
- Count fixed pawns on the color of the bishops and available outposts for knights.
- Move the king toward the sector where your minor piece is strongest.
- Avoid pawn moves that create targets for the enemy piece while improving none of your own.
- When ahead, fix pawns on squares your piece attacks well and the opponent's piece defends poorly.

*A knight improves when the structure gives it protected anchor points.*

In minor-piece endings, the right route often matters more than the immediate tactic. A well-placed knight can freeze pawns, block kings, and create forks that never fully disappear from the position.

## Model Position 2: A bishop loves targets on its color complex

<chess-board fen="8/8/2k5/3p4/3P4/2B5/4K3/8 w - - 0 1" highlight="b4,c5,d4,e3" arrows="c3-a5,a5-d8"></chess-board>
*The bishop's strength grows when pawns become fixed and reachable.*

A bishop can feel bad in the middlegame and excellent in the ending if the structure opens. That is why endgame judgment must track future piece quality, not only current activity.

## Common Mistakes
- Calling knights good in closed positions without checking whether the outposts are real.
- Trading into a bishop ending while fixing all your pawns on the bishop's bad color.
- Leaving the king inactive while comparing minor pieces abstractly.
- Ignoring opposition concepts even when only one minor piece remains.

## Training Work
- Practice bishop versus knight endgames from both sides with a small number of pawns.
- Review your endgames and write one sentence on which minor piece was actually better and why.
- Study endings where a king march transformed a modest piece edge into a full point.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Minor-piece endings are strategic endgames. The winner is usually the player who understood the long-term squares earlier.
