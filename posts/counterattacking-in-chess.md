Counterattack is one of the most practical defensive tools in chess. It works because attacks often overcommit pieces to one area. If you can create a real threat elsewhere, the attacker may be forced to slow down, trade, or abandon the initiative completely.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Recognize when the opponent's attack leaves their own position under-defended.
- Use central counterplay, tactical threats, and active defense instead of waiting.
- Distinguish between a sound counterattack and a desperate random threat.
- Know when counterplay is stronger than pure consolidation.

## Practical Framework
- Look for the attacker's neglected king, center, or loose pieces.
- A good counterattack should create a problem the opponent must answer now, not later.
- Prefer counterplay that develops your pieces or opens lines for them.
- If you cannot equal the attack directly, change the subject of the position.

## Model Position 1: Meet a wing attack with a central strike

*Counterplay is strongest when it hits the center or the king immediately.*

If the opponent spends time on a flank operation, the center may become the softest point in the position. That is where counterattack often lives.

## Model Position 2: Active pieces are defensive resources

<chess-board fen="r2q1rk1/pp2bppp/2n1pn2/2bp4/3P4/2NBPN2/PPQ2PPP/R1B2RK1 w - - 0 10" highlight="c5,d4,g2" arrows="c3-a4,c5-d4"></chess-board>
*A threat against the attacker's structure can slow the attack more than a passive guard move.*

Counterattacks do not have to be mating threats. Often a threat to win a pawn, invade a file, or expose the enemy king is enough to rebalance the game.

## Common Mistakes
- Trying to counterattack when the opponent already has a forced sequence.
- Making threats that do not require an answer.
- Ignoring your own king safety while dreaming of active play.
- Refusing to defend at all when the position still needs one precise consolidating move.

## Training Work
- Review games where a direct attack failed because the defender hit back in the center.
- In your own losses, ask whether there was an active resource instead of the passive move you played.
- Practice positions from the defensive side and look for forcing replies, not just safe ones.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Counterattack is not a gamble. It is the art of making the attacker respect your threats before their plan becomes decisive.
