Tactics win games. Period. Even the most strategic game usually ends because one player missed a tactical blow. To get better, you don't just need to calculate; you need to **recognize patterns.**

## 🍴 1. The Fork

The most common tactic. A single piece (usually a knight or pawn) attacks two pieces at once.

<chess-board fen="rnbqkbnr/pppp1ppp/8/4N3/4P3/8/PPPP1PPP/RNBQKB1R b KQkq - 0 1" arrows="e5-f7,e5-d7"></chess-board>
*The "Royal Fork": Attacking the King and Queen at the same time.*

---

## 📍 2. The Pin

As the saying goes, "The pin is mightier than the sword." A piece is pinned when it cannot move because it would expose a more valuable piece behind it.

<chess-board fen="r1bqk1nr/pppp1ppp/2n5/1B2p3/4P3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/RNBQK2R w KQkq - 0 1" highlight="c6,b5" arrows="b5-c6"></chess-board>
*In the Ruy Lopez, White pins the knight on c6 to the king on e8.*

---

## 🍢 3. The Skewer

The "reverse pin." You attack a valuable piece, and when it moves, you capture the less valuable piece behind it.

<chess-board fen="8/8/8/8/8/2B5/k7/r3K3 w - - 0 1" arrows="c3-a1"></chess-board>
*The bishop skewers the king to the rook.*

---

## 🌬️ 4. The Windmill

A devastating sequence where a series of discovered checks allows you to sweep up half the opponent's board. (Most famous example: Carlos Torre vs. Emanuel Lasker, 1925).

---

## 🚀 How to Improve Your Tactical Vision:

1. **Solve by Pattern:** Don't just do "random" puzzles. Do 50 "forks," then 50 "pins," then 50 "back-rank mates."
2. **The "Check-Capture-Threat" Scan:** On every move, scan the board for these three things.
3. **Analyze Your Tactical Blunders:** If you miss a tactic in a game, use a "Spaced Repetition" system to make sure you never miss that specific pattern again.

> 💡 **Remember:** Tactics are 99% of chess at the club level. If you stop blundering pieces and start spotting simple forks, you'll gain 200 rating points in a month.

**Sharpen your eyes and prepare to strike!**
