Tournament preparation works best when it reduces uncertainty. The point is not to feel dramatic or deeply prepared in every line. The point is to arrive with a warm calculation engine, clear opening choices, enough energy, and a repeatable between-round routine.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Prepare the day before, the hour before, and the minutes before the round differently.
- Focus on likely openings and practical reminders instead of trying to memorize everything.
- Manage food, rest, notation materials, and emotional energy like competitive resources.
- Use between-round recovery so one result does not poison the next game.

## Practical Framework
- Before the event, refresh your main repertoire and play a few training positions in your key structures.
- On the day, warm up with calculation and visualization rather than opening overload.
- If you know the opponent, prepare practical choices, not an entire database dump.
- Between rounds, recover first and analyze second unless the game produced a crucial opening lesson.

## Model Position 1: Preparation means knowing your likely choices

*Even before round one, you should know your practical answers to the main first moves.*

Good preparation reduces decision fatigue. If your broad opening choices are already settled, you save energy for the positions that actually demand calculation at the board.

## Model Position 2: Review plans, not just moves

<chess-board fen="r1bqkb1r/pp3ppp/2p1pn2/3p4/2PP4/2N2N2/PP2PPPP/R1BQKB1R w KQkq - 0 5" highlight="d4,c4,e5" arrows="c4-d5,c6-d5"></chess-board>
*A tournament note about structure is often more useful than a longer move tree.*

If your notes tell you where the pieces belong and what break matters, you can survive imperfect memory. If your notes are only memorized moves, one sideline can erase the value of the whole file.

## Common Mistakes
- Staying up late memorizing lines instead of protecting sleep and concentration.
- Preparing every possible opening and arriving mentally tired.
- Eating and hydrating carelessly across a long event.
- Analyzing a painful loss for an hour when recovery is what the next round needs.

## Training Work
- Build a tournament checklist that covers materials, food, sleep, openings, and warm-up.
- Create one-page opening summaries for events instead of long notes you will never review properly.
- After each tournament, refine the routine based on what actually helped and what wasted energy.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Tournament preparation is a system, not a burst of panic. The calmer and narrower the system, the more useful it becomes over many rounds.
