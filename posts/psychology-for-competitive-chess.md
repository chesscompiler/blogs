Chess is played between two humans, not two computers. This means nerves, fear, overconfidence, and "tilt" are just as important as opening theory.

## 😤 1. Handling "Tilt"

"Tilt" is when a bad result (or a bad move) makes you play worse.
- **The Remedy:** If you make a blunder, take 30 seconds to breathe. The move is done. You are now playing a *new* position. Don't let the previous mistake dictate your next one.

## 🎭 2. Playing "The Man," Not Just the Board

Is your opponent aggressive? They might be prone to overextending. Are they very solid? They might be afraid of a "messy" position.

- **Objective Truth vs. Practical Pressure:** Sometimes the "best" move is less effective than a "tricky" move that puts your opponent in time pressure or forces them to make a difficult psychological choice.

## ⏲️ 3. Managing Time Pressure

Time pressure is a psychological weapon.
- **Don't save all your time for the endgame:** If you make a mistake in the opening because you were "speeding," you might never reach the endgame.
- **Trust your intuition:** In a time scramble, you don't have time to calculate everything. Trust your pattern recognition.

---

## 🛡️ 4. The "Resigned" Mindset

Never assume a game is won until the handshake.
- **The "Winner's Curse":** Being up material can lead to laziness. "I'm winning anyway, I don't need to calculate this line." This is exactly when your opponent will find a saving trick.

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** Before a tournament, focus on your **process**, not your **result**. If you focus on playing good moves, the rating points will follow. If you focus on the points, you'll play with fear.

---

## 📋 The Competitive Mindset Checklist:
- [ ] Did I sleep well?
- [ ] Am I hydrated?
- [ ] Have I accepted that I might lose? (Accepting the worst outcome can paradoxically help you play more freely).
- [ ] Am I ready to fight for 4 hours?

**Chess is a battle of wills. Make sure yours is the stronger one!**
