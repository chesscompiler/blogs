The middlegame feels difficult because the position rarely tells you one move by force. Planning solves that problem. A plan is simply a move sequence tied to a real positional fact: a weak square, a space edge, an open file, a bad piece, or a king target.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Build plans from the structure and piece placement instead of from vague preferences.
- Identify the most important imbalance before choosing a move.
- Know when to improve your worst piece, when to trade, and when to open the position.
- Use short plans that can be revised after the next tactical change.

## Practical Framework
- Describe the position first: who has more space, safer king, better minor piece, and healthier structure.
- Choose one target and one route to increase pressure on it.
- If no concrete action is available, improve your least active piece.
- Re-evaluate after every pawn break because middlegame plans change when the structure changes.

*A quiet improving move often prepares the real break later.*

Club players often attack before their pieces are coordinated. Stronger players first ask which piece still has no useful job. Fixing that piece makes the later tactical opportunities real.

## Common Mistakes
- Copying a plan from another opening without checking whether the structure matches.
- Attacking a target that can be defended easily while ignoring a more static weakness elsewhere.
- Making a long-term plan and then refusing to abandon it when tactics change the board.
- Forgetting that exchanges should serve the plan, not just reduce tension.

## Training Work
- Take ten middlegame positions from master games and write one sentence describing the main imbalance.
- In your own games, annotate the first move where you were no longer following a clear plan.
- Practice finding three candidate plans before choosing a move in strategic positions.

## Why This Chapter Matters
A useful middlegame plan is grounded in something visible. If you cannot name the structural reason behind the plan, it is probably not a plan yet.
