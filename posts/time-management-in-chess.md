The clock punishes poor process as much as poor moves. Good time management is not about moving fast everywhere. It is about spending time where the position demands it and conserving time where understanding should already carry the load.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Divide the game into phases with different time demands.
- Recognize genuine critical moments before the clock forces the issue.
- Avoid early time sinks caused by weak opening preparation or indecisive candidate selection.
- Use practical decision rules in time trouble without collapsing into blunders.

## Practical Framework
- Know your opening well enough that the first ten moves do not cost half your clock.
- Spend time when the structure is changing, when tactics are sharp, or when one decision will define the game plan.
- When there is no critical decision, choose the best solid move from a small candidate set and keep time in reserve.
- In time trouble, prioritize king safety, forcing moves, and simple positions you can still handle.

If you know the structure and the plan, spending five extra minutes here rarely buys anything. Save the deep think for the moment where the position actually leaves known territory.

*A central break can change the entire game, so the clock should reflect that.*

Critical moments often involve irreversible pawn moves, tactical forcing sequences, or exchanges into a different endgame. Those are the moments where deep time investment makes sense.

## Common Mistakes
- Thinking too long in familiar positions and too little in changing ones.
- Looking for the perfect move in a position that only asks for a good practical move.
- Entering time trouble because you never defined a candidate-move process.
- Playing instantly in sharp positions just because the clock is low.

## Training Work
- Review your tournament games and mark the move where time trouble started, not just where it exploded.
- Practice rapid games with a written post-game note on which positions deserved more or less time.
- Use internal checkpoints such as move 15 and move 25 to assess clock health during games.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Strong clock use comes from clear thinking habits. The better your process, the more time you seem to have.
