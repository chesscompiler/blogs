Prophylaxis is the discipline of improving your position while limiting the opponent's best idea. It feels subtle because the move often looks modest on the board. In practice, it is one of the clearest markers of mature chess understanding.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Ask what the opponent wants before deciding what you want.
- Recognize common prophylactic moves such as luft, restraining a pawn break, or taking away an entry square.
- Use restraint without becoming passive.
- See how prophylaxis and active play work together rather than against each other.

## Practical Framework
- Before every move, identify the opponent's most useful plan if they got two moves in a row.
- Choose the smallest move that reduces that plan while keeping your own options open.
- Prophylaxis is strongest when it improves a piece and limits the opponent at the same time.
- If a position has no forcing action, prophylaxis often becomes the best practical use of a tempo.

The move h3 may not look dramatic, but if it removes a tactical idea or gains a safe square for the king, it can be the move that makes your future plan possible.

## Model Position 2: Creating luft before it is urgent

<chess-board fen="6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/5PPP/5RK1 w - - 0 1" highlight="h2,h3,g2" arrows="h2-h3"></chess-board>
*Defending against a pattern before it exists is often easier than repairing it later.*

Back-rank problems are a perfect example of prophylaxis. A quiet pawn move prevents future tactical disasters and gives your heavy pieces more freedom.

## Common Mistakes
- Playing prophylactic moves that solve no real problem.
- Using restraint as an excuse to avoid active decisions.
- Ignoring your opponent's plan until it becomes an immediate threat.
- Forgetting to re-check the position after the opponent changes plans.

## Training Work
- Annotate master games and circle the moves that reduced the opponent's plan before improving the player's own.
- In your own games, write down one move where you should have asked what the opponent wanted.
- Practice positions with no tactics and choose the move that limits the opponent the most.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Prophylaxis is practical humility. It accepts that your opponent has ideas too, and it builds your position with that fact in mind.
