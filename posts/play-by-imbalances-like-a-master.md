Strong players do not ask for the best move in a vacuum. They ask which imbalance matters most. Material, space, pawn structure, initiative, king safety, and minor-piece quality all point toward different plans. Your job is to rank them correctly.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Identify the major imbalance before calculating candidate moves.
- Understand how one imbalance can outweigh another, such as activity outweighing material or space outweighing structure.
- Use imbalances to explain why certain exchanges help one side more than the other.
- Translate abstract assessment into a practical plan.

## Practical Framework
- List the big imbalances in order of importance, not all at the same level.
- Choose a move that strengthens your favorable imbalance or reduces the opponent's.
- If the position changes sharply, redo the imbalance list instead of carrying over an old evaluation.
- Use imbalances to simplify your calculation tree: many moves become irrelevant once the real strategic fight is clear.

## Model Position 1: Space plus better knight beats cosmetic symmetry

*An outpost can matter more than a perfectly healthy pawn structure.*

Players often overvalue static neatness. If one side owns more useful space and a superior minor piece, that dynamic edge may dominate the position for many moves.

## Model Position 2: Material is only one imbalance

<chess-board fen="r2q1rk1/pp2bppp/2n1pn2/2bp4/3P4/2NBPN2/PPQ2PPP/R1B2RK1 w - - 0 10" highlight="c5,e6,f7" arrows="c3-d5,e6-d5"></chess-board>
*Even a material deficit can be playable if the activity is severe enough.*

This is why exchange sacrifices and pawn sacrifices appear so often in high-level chess. The board evaluates relationships, not just arithmetic.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating every factor as equally important in every position.
- Grabbing material that kills your own activity.
- Ignoring king safety because the material count looks favorable.
- Thinking in labels such as good bishop or bad bishop without checking the concrete squares involved.

## Training Work
- Pause master games at move fifteen and write an imbalance list before seeing the next move.
- Review positions you misjudged and ask which imbalance you ranked incorrectly.
- Explain your move choice in one sentence using the word because.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Imbalance thinking turns vague strategic language into practical direction. It tells you what the position is really about.
