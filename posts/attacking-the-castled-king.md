Most failed attacks against a castled king share the same flaw: the attacker started before the position justified it. A successful king hunt needs a stable center, enough attacking pieces, and a way to open lines without getting mated first.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Recognize the structural and developmental signs that an attack can succeed.
- Use pawn storms, hooks, piece lifts, and sacrifice themes with the right timing.
- Count attackers versus defenders before committing.
- Understand how opposite-side castling changes the value of time.

## Practical Framework
- Check the center first. If the center is unstable, a wing attack may simply expose your own king.
- Bring pieces before pawns when the files are not ready to open.
- Look for hooks such as h6, g6, or f6 that give your pawns contact points.
- Do not confuse one attractive motif with a complete attack. The attack must survive the opponent's best defense.

## Model Position 1: Pawn storms need a clear target

*The attack becomes real when the pawn shield already contains a hook.*

The point of a pawn storm is not just to gain space. It is to force contact, open files, and bring heavy pieces to the weakened king. Without that contact, the storm is just overextension.

## Model Position 2: The Greek Gift depends on support

<chess-board fen="r1bq1rk1/ppp1nppp/2n1p3/3pP3/1b1P4/2NB1N2/PPP2PPP/R1BQK2R w KQ - 3 7" highlight="h7,g5,h5" arrows="d3-h7,f3-g5,d1-h5"></chess-board>
*A bishop sacrifice works only when the rest of the army can flood in.*

This pattern is famous because it is vivid, but the underlying lesson is sober: calculate the defensive resources first. If the defenders are still coordinated, the sacrifice is only dramatic, not correct.

## Common Mistakes
- Launching pawns before enough pieces can join the attack.
- Attacking on the wing while the center can be blown open against your own king.
- Forgetting defensive resources such as returning material or trading queens.
- Counting one sacrifice as the whole combination instead of the beginning of it.

## Training Work
- Study attacking games where the center was closed or stable enough to allow wing play.
- Review your own attacks and mark the move where you started before your pieces were ready.
- Practice counting attackers and defenders out loud before entering tactical lines.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Strong attacks on a castled king are built, not improvised. The preconditions matter as much as the final tactical shot.
