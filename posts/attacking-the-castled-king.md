The king has castled, tucked safely behind a wall of pawns. You've developed your pieces. Now, how do you break through? Attacking is not just about aggression; it's about **patterns**.

## 🎁 The Greek Gift (Bxh7+)

One of the most famous sacrifices in chess. If your opponent has a knight on f6 and you have a bishop on d3, a knight on f3, and a queen ready to jump into action, the Greek Gift might be on the table.

<chess-board fen="r1bq1rk1/ppp1bppp/2np1n2/4p3/2BPP3/2N2N2/PPP2PPP/R1BQ1RK1 w - - 0 1" arrows="c4-f7,f3-g5,d1-h5"></chess-board>
*Warning: Always calculate if the king can escape via e7 before sacrificing!*

## ⚓ The Hook Mate

A hook mate uses a pawn and a piece (usually a rook or queen) to trap the king against his own defensive structure.

<chess-board fen="6k1/5p1p/6p1/R7/8/8/8/6K1 w - - 0 1" highlight="g6,h7,f7" arrows="a5-a8"></chess-board>
*Notice how the g6 pawn "hooks" the king, preventing escape.*

---

## 💨 The Smothered Mate

The ultimate tactical humiliation. A lone knight delivers mate because the king's own pieces are blocking every exit.

<chess-board fen="6rk/5Npp/8/8/8/8/8/7K b - - 0 1" highlight="h8,g8,g7"></chess-board>
*A classic finish. To achieve this, you often need to sacrifice a Queen on g8 first!*

---

## 🗝️ Three Rules for a Successful Attack

1. **Bring all the boys to the party:** An attack with only two pieces usually fails. You need coordination.
2. **Open the lines:** Use pawn breaks to open files for your rooks and diagonals for your bishops.
3. **Eliminate the key defender:** Often, the knight on f6 (for Black) or f3 (for White) is the "anchor" of the defense. Trade it off or drive it away!

> ⚠️ **Common Mistake:** Attacking before you've finished development. If your rooks are still in the corners, your attack lacks "ammo."
