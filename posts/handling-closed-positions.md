Closed positions frustrate players who only look for immediate tactics. With no open lines, the game becomes a test of patience, maneuvering, pawn breaks, and long-term square control. That does not make the position passive; it makes the preparation more important.

## What This Chapter Teaches
- Recognize when a closed center shifts the game to maneuvering and wing play.
- Improve knights, reroute bishops, and prepare pawn breaks without loosening your own structure.
- Identify which side of the board you should actually play on.
- Know when to keep tension and when to open the structure yourself.

## Practical Framework
- In closed positions, ask which pawn break each side wants and how many moves it needs.
- Knights usually gain value and bishops often need rerouting or exchange support.
- Do not open the position until your pieces are ready to benefit from the change.
- Space is an asset only if you use it to improve piece placement and restrict counterplay.

*A knight reroute can matter more than a direct attack.*

When the center is locked, good moves often look slow. That is normal. The hidden purpose is to place pieces where they will support the eventual break or dominate key squares.

## Model Position 2: Prepare the break before you push

<chess-board fen="r2q1rk1/pp1nbppp/2pp1n2/4p3/2PPP3/2N1BN2/PP3PPP/R2Q1RK1 w - - 0 8" highlight="b4,c5,e5" arrows="b2-b4,a2-a4"></chess-board>
*Wing breaks work when the pieces behind them are already coordinated.*

Many attacks in closed positions fail because the pawns run ahead of the pieces. Good maneuvering means the break is the last step of the plan, not the first.

## Common Mistakes
- Calling a position dead just because no tactics are available yet.
- Opening the center while your king is less safe than the opponent's.
- Leaving bishops behind bad pawn chains without a rerouting plan.
- Pushing both wings and weakening everything at once.

## Training Work
- Study classic games from players known for maneuvering in closed positions.
- Take one closed structure you play often and write the standard knight routes for both sides.
- Play training games where you are not allowed to make a pawn break until you can explain why it helps.

## Why This Chapter Matters
Closed positions reward patience with purpose. If you know the break you are preparing, the quiet moves stop feeling aimless.
