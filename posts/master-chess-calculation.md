Calculation is the "engine" of your chess skill. You can know all the strategy in the world, but if you calculate a line three moves deep and miss a back-rank mate, you lose.

## 🚦 The "Candidate Moves" Method

Before you start "calculating," you must "list." Alexander Kotov, in *Think Like a Grandmaster*, suggested identifying 2-3 **Candidate Moves** first.

1. **Checks:** Every possible check.
2. **Captures:** Every possible capture.
3. **Threats:** Moves that create a direct threat (like attacking a Queen).

## 🌲 The Calculation Tree

Once you have your candidates, calculate them one by one.
- Go as deep as you can on the most "forcing" line first.
- If a line leads to a clear advantage, you might be able to stop and play it.
- If it leads to a disaster, discard it and move to the next candidate.

---

## 👁️ Improving Visualization

Visualization is the ability to see the board in your head 3, 5, or 10 moves ahead.
- **Blindfold Practice:** Try to follow a game score (e.g., 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3...) in your head without a board.
- **The "Final Position" Check:** Once you finish a calculation, look at the *imagined* final position. Is the king safe? Is anything hanging?

---

## ⚠️ Common Pitfalls

- **Blunder Checking:** Always do a "one-move blunder check" before you actually move the piece.
- **Wishful Thinking:** "If I play this, he'll probably play that, and then I win." No! Assume your opponent will play the **best** move.
- **Stopping Too Early:** Many tactical shots are missed because a player stops calculating one move before the "punchline."

> 💡 **Training Tip:** Solve "Hard" puzzles where the solution is 5+ moves deep. Don't move the pieces until you see the whole line!

**Sharpen your calculation, and you'll find yourself winning games that used to be "messy" or "unclear."**
