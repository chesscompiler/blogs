Strong players do not "guess" moves—they calculate with structure. If you want fewer blunders and more confident decisions, build a repeatable calculation process.

> 💡 **Core idea:** Calculate *candidate moves first*, then analyze forcing lines in a disciplined order.

## 1) Build 2–3 Candidate Moves
Before calculating deeply, list reasonable options:
- A forcing move (check/capture/threat)
- A positional improving move
- A defensive/prophylactic move

In this structure, White can consider pressure on f7, central expansion, or slow improvement.

## 2) Use Forcing-Move Priority
For each candidate, analyze in this order:
1. Checks
2. Captures
3. Threats

This reduces missed tactics because forcing lines narrow the tree.

## 3) Stop at Stable Positions
Don’t calculate forever. Stop when:
- Tactics are resolved
- Piece safety is clear
- Pawn structure is understood
- You can evaluate better/worse/equal

## 4) Practical Training Plan
- Solve 5 timed puzzles daily (3–5 min each)
- Solve 2 deep puzzles weekly (15+ min)
- Annotate one game with full candidate-move notes

If you keep this process for 6–8 weeks, your board vision and conversion rate improve dramatically.
