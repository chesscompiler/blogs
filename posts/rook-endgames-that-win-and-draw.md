Rook endgames are the most common endgames in chess. If you want to improve your rating, you *must* master the two most fundamental positions: the Lucena and the Philidor.

## 🌉 The Lucena Position: The "Bridge"

The Lucena position is the blueprint for winning when you have a Rook and a Pawn vs. a Rook.

**The Goal:** To get your king out of the path of your pawn so it can promote.

<chess-board fen="2K5/4P3/8/8/8/5r2/8/2k2R2 w - - 0 1" arrows="f1-f4,f4-d4,d4-d7"></chess-board>
*Key Move: 1. Rf4! This is the start of building "The Bridge."*

1.  Check the enemy king away from the action.
2.  Move your rook to the 4th rank.
3.  Use your king to shelter the pawn, using the rook as a "bridge" against enemy checks.

---

## 🛑 The Philidor Position: The Ultimate Defense

The Philidor position is how you hold a draw when you are down a pawn.

**The Strategy:** Keep your rook on the 3rd rank until the opponent's pawn reaches that rank, then move your rook to the back rank to check the king from behind.

<chess-board fen="4k3/8/2rP4/8/8/8/5K2/7R b - - 0 1" arrows="c6-c1,c1-h1,h1-h2"></chess-board>
*Once the pawn reaches d6, Black plays Rc1 and starts checking the White king from the rear.*

---

## 📏 General Rules for Rook Endgames:

1.  **Rooks belong behind passed pawns.** Whether it's your pawn (to support it) or your opponent's (to stop it).
2.  **Activity is everything.** Sometimes it's better to sacrifice a pawn to keep your rook active rather than becoming passive and defending.
3.  **King position matters.** In the endgame, the king is an attacking piece. Bring him to the center!

> 💡 **Training Tip:** Set up these positions against a computer. If you can't win the Lucena or hold the Philidor 10 times out of 10, keep practicing!

**Master these, and you'll stop "guessing" in the endgame and start playing with Grandmaster precision.**
